# Volume I — Learning Philosophy

Learning is a change in what a person can do later, in a new item of the same class, without us standing next to them.

**Learning as transformation**  
A completed screen is not a transformation. Transformation is a new capability that survives a gap. We measure the later try, not the applause at the end of the clip.

**Mastery over completion**  
Completion is an event. Mastery is stability across spaced transfer items (Bloom, 1968; Product Architecture states). A path that only marks “done” is a content platform. We are not that.

**Understanding before memorization**  
Facts may be retrieved. They are not the first god. A term without a use is a load, not a skill. Memorization is a servant of fluency, not a substitute for a model of the idea (Bransford, Brown & Cocking, *How People Learn*).

**Curiosity before compliance**  
A question they ask is honored. A question we force for a metric is load. Compliance can produce a quiet classroom and an empty mind. Curiosity is not chaos. It is a chosen next try (Deci & Ryan, self-determination: autonomy).

**Learning through practice**  
The learner produces first when the goal is skill (Roediger & Karpicke). We do not lecture them into a feeling of competence.

**Learning through reflection**  
After the set, one beat: what was hard (Flavell, metacognition). During the try, no journal. Reflection that steals working memory is not reflection.

**Human-centered education**  
The person is not a node in our graph. Age, language, fatigue, and dignity outrank a pacing algorithm. A human teacher may override the machine. The machine may not override a child’s safety.

**Lifelong learning**  
A session dies. A life of practice does not. Forgotten is a scheduling fact. Return after years is placement, not a stranger greeting.

**Adaptive learning**  
Difficulty, spacing, and next item adapt from licensed attempts. Identity does not. Adaptation that surprises them with a biography they did not give is not adaptive. It is surveillance.

**Learning with dignity**  
A miss is information. It is not a verdict on the soul. No rank among children. No streak as love. No cute shame. The same respect for a child, an elder, and a tired teacher.

# Volume II — Learning Architecture

The permanent model of what grows. Every learner progresses through these, not through a feed.

**Knowledge**  
What can be pointed at: a fact, a definition, a source. Stored in the knowledge bucket, not in a person’s secret diary.

**Concepts**  
Atomic ideas a person can be right or wrong about. The unit of first encoding.

**Skills**  
Observable performances composed of concepts (solve a two-step equation aloud; write a paragraph that argues). The unit of practice.

**Competencies**  
Clusters of skills that transfer across items and, later, across contexts. A competency is not a badge name. It is a class of performance a teacher can sample.

**Habits**  
Repeated procedures that free working memory (check the units; read the question twice). Taught as short strategies, then faded. Not a personality program.

**Understanding**  
A usable model of a concept: they can explain, choose an example, and reject a near-miss. Understanding precedes fluency as a goal; fluency without a model is a trick.

**Fluency**  
Accurate performance with low enough load that attention can move to the next demand (Bindra / automaticity literature; classroom: they can do the step and still hear the next). Fluency is practiced. It is not rushed on day one.

**Transfer**  
Success on a new item of the same class, later (Perkins & Salomon). Near transfer is the exam. Far transfer is the world. We do not claim far transfer from one identical drill.

**Mastery**  
Stable success across spaced transfer items. Can decay. Returns to Needs Revision without drama.

**Lifelong growth**  
New domains, new competencies, old ones maintained. The graph lengthens. The person is not reset.

## How a learner progresses

Not Started → first encoding (concept) → practice at the edge (skill) → fluency on the class → transfer sample → mastery state → maintain or teach → decay → revise.

The Planner picks the next move on this chain.  
A feature that skips practice and calls itself mastery is a lie.  
A feature that never maintains will mint false masters.

# Volume III — Curriculum Architecture

Curriculum is how knowledge is packaged for teaching.  
It is not the product map.  
It is not a guest personality.

**Domains** — Stable fields (mathematics, language). Outlive a board’s syllabus year.

**Subjects** — Taught slices (Class 8 Mathematics; Spoken Hindi). May follow a board. Must still map to the graph.

**Courses** — A timed offering of a subject (this term, this cohort). Has a start and an end. The person’s graph does not end when the course does.

**Units** — A cluster of related objectives inside a course.

**Modules** — A teachable sequence inside a unit. Small enough to finish without a festival.

**Lessons** — One guided encode plus one check. Short. Voice-first when the skill is spoken.

**Concepts / Skills** — Bound to lessons and items. A lesson that cannot name them is content, not curriculum.

**Learning objectives**  
Observable, one class of performance, testable by an item. “Appreciate” is not an objective. “Solve,” “explain,” “retrieve,” “pronounce” are.

**Outcomes**  
What a teacher or parent can repeat: the competency sampled, not a vibe.

**Competency maps**  
Competencies → skills → concepts, with prerequisite edges. The Planner reads this. The mouth does not recite it.

**Learning paths**  
Spaced sequences from first encoding to maintain. Assignable. Not shaming. A path may be left. Dignity does not require finishing our sequence today.

A guest without a subgraph of this architecture is a costume.  
A marketplace of courses that do not map to concepts and skills is a store, not an OS.

# Volume IV — Learning Journey

Product Architecture named a spine. This volume is the educational lifecycle and every transition.

Discover → Understand → Practice → Apply → Recall → Assess → Reflect → Improve → Master → Teach → Maintain

**Discover → Understand**  
They have a goal, a gap, or an assignment. Transition when one concept is chosen. Not when a catalog is browsed.

**Understand → Practice**  
First encoding happened (a model or a definition they can use). Transition when they can attempt a step, not when we finished talking.

**Practice → Apply**  
Accuracy at the edge on isomorphic items. Transition when we change the costume of the item (new numbers, new sentence) and they still perform.

**Apply → Recall**  
A gap has passed (Cepeda et al.). Transition is scheduled, not mood-based. We do not re-lecture to “be helpful.”

**Recall → Assess**  
They have produced from memory. A sample of the class may now be disclosed as assessment.

**Assess → Reflect**  
The sample ended. One beat: what was hard. Not a character sketch.

**Reflect → Improve**  
The miss or the weak class becomes the next item. Difficulty is visible (Ericsson: edge of ability).

**Improve → Master**  
Spaced transfer holds. State becomes Mastered.

**Master → Teach**  
Optional. They explain to a peer, a younger learner, or a written proof (protégé effect; Bargh & Schul; nested in Vygotsky’s social line). Never required to keep dignity.

**Teach → Maintain / Master → Maintain**  
Maintenance is spaced return. Mastery without maintain is a trophy on a melting shelf.

**Any stage → Recovering**  
Miss, drop, fail-closed: same thread, smaller demand. Not a new journey.

**Any stage → Archived**  
Removed from the active path. History they own remains.

A product may start mid-journey (a teacher assigns Practice). It may not invent a parallel journey called “engagement.”

# Volume V — Skill Architecture

A skill is an observable performance. If we cannot name the performance, we do not have a skill. We have a slogan.

**Hard skills**  
Domain performances with closed or semi-closed items: calculate, parse, conjugate, code a function, name a structure. Guests may drill these. Tools may verify closed parts.

**Soft skills**  
Performances in human situations: ask a clear question, disagree without contempt, listen, turn-take. Taught with scenarios and oral practice. Not scored by a tool as a soul. Not a hidden personality grade.

**Meta skills**  
Performances about learning: plan a set, choose a hint, notice a miss, schedule a return (Weinstein, Acee & Jung: strategic learning). Taught rarely, in short strategies, then used. Not a course that replaces the subject.

**Communication**  
Speak, listen, write, read, and switch language with dignity. Native script. Code-switch allowed. This skill class is first-class in a voice OS.

**Critical thinking**  
Reject a near-miss, ask for a source, check a step. Taught on real items, not as a poster of “think harder.”

**Creativity**  
Produce a new instance of a class (a new example, a new sentence, a new approach). Bloom’s create is slow. Voice may start it. It is not completed by a spoken turn alone (Operating Standards: Bloom).

**Collaboration**  
Work with a peer or a teacher in a room that has presence. No silent observers. A dropped peer fails toward the host.

**Problem solving**  
Represent, plan, try, check. Polya’s shape, not a ritual speech. The Planner may hold the next move. The learner still owns the try.

**Digital literacy**  
Use the OS without being used: permissions, forget, search as prosthetic, know that a fluent sentence can be wrong.

**AI literacy**  
Know the host is a teacher-seat, a guest is named, uncertainty is a hedge, a tool can fail. Not a course in our architecture for its own glory. A few acts: see Memory, see a guest leave, hear “I am not sure.”

Soft and meta skills never become a secret dossier. They may be practiced. They are not a rank.

# Volume VI — Assessment Architecture

Assessment samples a class of skill. It scores the attempt, not the person.

**Diagnostic**  
Before a path: what is already there. Short. Placement, not a verdict. Used to skip or to descend. Not used to shame a returner.

**Formative**  
During learning: the next item is the feedback (Black & Wiliam). Voice checks are formative by default. They do not enter a permanent record unless a human path says so.

**Summative**  
After a stretch: a disclosed sample of the class. The person knows it is a sample. Results go to the outcome they were told — teacher, parent projection, or only themselves.

**Competency assessment**  
Several skills of one competency, including transfer items. One identical drill is not a competency.

**Performance tasks**  
A longer doing: a proof, a spoken paragraph, a lab-like explanation. Rubrics. Judgment of a child is not a tool’s alone.

**Projects**  
Across sessions. Still items and criteria. Not a portfolio costume that stores a tape.

**Oral assessment**  
First-class in this OS. Same rules: disclose, score the attempt, no feed, human path if official.

**AI-supported assessment**  
Tools score closed tasks. AI may draft a rubric note for a teacher. AI does not publish a child’s character. AI does not own the official record.

**Human review**  
Required when the record is permanent, high-stakes, or the task is judgment. The teacher’s override stands.

**Certification**  
A claim to the world. Allowed only with a human path, a disclosed sample, and a competency — not a streak. We do not mint certificates for sitting through a guest.

An assessment that cannot fail closed to a human review does not enter a permanent record.

# Volume VII — Practice Architecture

Practice is the kernel of learning. Features that do not produce a try are not practice.

**Active recall**  
A blank or a question before a reveal (generation / testing effect). Flashcards and voice drills obey this or they are content.

**Spaced repetition**  
Weak items return after a gap (Ebbinghaus; Cepeda et al.). Streaks exist to serve spacing, not the reverse.

**Interleaving**  
Mix related item types after initial encoding (Rohrer). Not on the first minute of a brand-new idea.

**Deliberate practice**  
One weakness, at the edge, with feedback, repeat (Ericsson). Difficulty is visible. A hidden mood engine is not deliberate.

**Retrieval practice**  
Produce from memory. The system does not re-lecture the answer to look smart (Roediger & Karpicke).

**Mixed practice**  
After encoding, vary the surface so the class, not the costume, is learned (desirable difficulties: Bjork).

**Scenario practice**  
Soft skills and application in a situation. Still one primary. Still a way to stop.

**Adaptive practice**  
Next item from last attempt and licensed state. Smaller isomorphic item on miss. Harder transfer on stability. Not a random “keep them busy.”

**Reflection practice**  
The one beat after a set. Stored only if licensed. Not during the try (Sweller: extraneous load).

**Transfer practice**  
New item of the same class, later. If we only ever repeat the identical item, we are training a trick.

Worked examples may start a new idea, then fade (Renkl; expertise reversal: Kalyuga). A worked example that never fades is a lecture.

# Volume VIII — Feedback Architecture

Feedback is information for the next try (Hattie & Timperley: feed up, back, and forward). It is not a show.

**Immediate feedback**  
On form and on a closed step: the next sentence. Voice lives here. Do not wait a day to say a sign error.

**Delayed feedback**  
On judgment they must still think through, or on a summative sample they must finish. Delay is pedagogical, not operational neglect.

**Hints**  
One. Point at the next operation or the forgotten definition. A hint that is the answer is the solution — say so, and only on request.

**Corrections**  
Name the miss. Do not name the person. Then a smaller try or the solution if they ask.

**Scaffolding**  
Visible, temporary support (Wood, Bruner & Ross; Vygotsky: ZPD). We say when it will fade. Expertise reversal: the same scaffold that helps a novice can harm a fluent learner (Kalyuga).

**Encouragement**  
The next try is possible. Not a slogan. Not false praise that ruins calibration.

**Reflection**  
After the set. One question. Stop.

**Error recovery**  
Name, smaller isomorphic item or one hint, same thread. Do not replace them with the full solution unless they ask (Volume I: dignity; Bjork: desirable difficulty — do not steal all difficulty).

**Motivation**  
Competence, autonomy, relatedness (Deci & Ryan). A line that harms one of the three is guilty. Points are not love.

**Mastery feedback**  
State the fact: this class held across a gap. No forever. Maintenance will return.

If feedback can be removed and the next try is still possible, consider removing it.

# Volume IX — Personalization Architecture

Personalization changes the next item, the pace, and the schedule.  
It does not change who we say they are.

**Difficulty adjustment**  
Edge of ability. Visible. After success, fade support and raise the class. After miss, smaller isomorphic item. Not a secret.

**Learning pace**  
Enough is a valid end. We do not trap. Spacing still schedules return. Pace is autonomy, not abandonment.

**Learning preferences**  
They may set language, voice or type, session length. We do not invent a “visual learner” doctrine as science (Pashler et al.: learning-styles hypothesis lacks evidence). We may offer modes. We do not lock a child into a myth.

**Goal adaptation**  
Goals they set can change. The OS does not invent a life plan. Exam dates they gave may tighten a path. They may still stop.

**Revision scheduling**  
Due from Forgotten and Needs Revision. Honest. Not a threat.

**Weakness detection**  
From attempts, not from a vibe or a face. Named as a class of skill. Shown to them and, if granted, to a teacher.

**Strength reinforcement**  
Fluency items may interleave so a strength stays warm. We do not park them in easy theater to protect a metric.

**Recommendation engine**  
The Planner is the recommender: one next move. A store of “you might also like” is not a learning recommender.

**Learning memory**  
States, last class scores, due dates, licensed fields. The only memory personalization may read.

**Human override**  
A teacher may assign, skip, lock, or pull a guest. A parent may not rewrite a teacher’s professional judgment by dashboard. A learner may stop. The OS does not sulk.

Personalization that requires extra surveillance is refused.

# Volume X — Learning Analytics

Analytics exist to improve the next try. They do not exist to rank people.

**Progress** — Where on the journey and in the states. Not a cartoon of a life.

**Mastery** — Count of classes in Mastered, and how many returned to Needs Revision. Decay is a first-class number.

**Retention** — Recall success after a gap. The north-star educational metric beside transfer.

**Engagement** — Not a learning state. If used at all, it means “completed a real attempt” — not time trapped, not taps, not smile time.

**Confidence** — Their stated or revealed calibration, compared to correctness. False high confidence is a teaching target, not a win.

**Learning velocity** — Change in a class over spaced samples. Not items per minute. Speed without retention is a vanity.

**Practice quality** — Attempts at the edge, hints faded, transfer items present. Not streak length.

**Goal completion** — Goals they set, honestly ended or met. Abandoned without shame.

**Knowledge gaps** — Prerequisites that fail when a later skill fails. For the Planner and the teacher.

**Transfer success** — New items of the class, later. If this number is missing, we do not claim mastery.

Rules:

- No leaderboard of children.
- No utterance in a chart.
- Empty is drawn.
- A metric that rises while the face falls is a failed metric (Operating Standards: Research).
- School and enterprise see aggregates and risk without gossip.
- The person can see the same educational facts we use to pace them, in language they can bear.

# Volume XI — Teacher Architecture

The teacher is a colleague. The OS is a tool in their hand, not a replacement of their judgment.

**Teacher dashboard**  
Pulse, risk, assignment, override. One primary per view. A line they can say to a parent in one breath. No tape. No gossip adjectives.

**Classroom support**  
Roster, presence that is visible, a dropped peer fails to host. No silent observers.

**Lesson planning**  
They bind a path, a unit, or an item to a class. The OS suggests from gaps. They decide. A plan is not a personality for the child.

**Intervention**  
Risk is a pattern of Forgotten, failed transfer, or distress — not a vibe. Intervention is human: assign a smaller class, talk, escalate. The OS does not message a child as a warden.

**Progress review**  
States and samples. They can open an item, not a mouth.

**Assignment management**  
Assign, due, collect outcomes. Oral and written. Clear when a set is formative or summative.

**AI collaboration**  
They may send a learner to a named guest through the host path. They may pull the guest. The assistant drafts; they own the send.

**Parent communication**  
Time, topic, next step, outcomes. The OS may draft. The teacher sends. No quote of the child.

**Human override**  
Stands. Difficulty, guest, record, “this is not the next item.” The OS does not sulk and does not re-route behind them.

**Professional judgment**  
Outranks the Planner on that learner in that class. Outranked only by safety and by the child’s dignity.

A teacher who cannot explain a student-facing line in one breath has been given a bad product, not a bad class.

# Volume XII — Parent Learning System

A parent is a care relationship. They are not a second examiner and not a warden of a tape.

**Progress visibility**  
Time, topics, completion, state in plain words. What is due. What was sampled if they are allowed to see it.

**Learning support**  
One next step they can do at a table: a smaller practice, a quiet place, a time. Not a script that turns home into a call center.

**Home practice**  
Optional assigned or suggested items. The child may still stop. Home is not a second shift of shame.

**Encouragement**  
The OS speaks to parents as adults: the next try is possible. It does not coach them to perform fake praise or to punish a miss.

**Learning environment**  
We may say: quieter, shorter, offline is honest. We do not inspect their house.

**Goal awareness**  
Goals the learner or teacher set, if sharing is granted. Parents may suggest. They do not silently rewrite a teacher’s assignment.

**Privacy boundaries**  
No eavesdrop. No utterance quote. No camera. Alerts are rare and about risk or safety, not a missed badge.

**Communication**  
A line they can repeat to another parent at a door. Language System applies.

**Shared responsibility**  
Learner tries. Teacher judges the class. Parent supports the conditions. The OS keeps the thread. None of the three owns the child’s mouth.

# Volume XIII — Educational Research

Every educational decision traces to evidence here, or to a written exception.

**Cognitive science**  
Working memory is limited (Baddeley). We present one clause, one number, one step. Dual-process load is not an excuse for a dashboard during a try.

**Learning sciences**  
People learn by doing, with feedback, in a community that does not humiliate (Bransford et al.; Lave & Wenger — community does not mean a public feed).

**Educational psychology**  
Prior knowledge is the strongest predictor (Ausubel). Diagnostics exist. We do not pretend a blank slate on return.

**Motivation research**  
Self-determination: competence, autonomy, relatedness (Deci & Ryan). Controlling rewards crowd out the try. Streak fear is a controlling reward.

**Neuroscience**  
We use it with humility. Spacing and sleep support consolidation (Walker; Diekelmann & Born). We do not sell “brain-based” decoration or face-reading as science.

**Memory research**  
Testing effect (Roediger & Karpicke). Spacing (Cepeda). Forgetting is normal (Ebbinghaus). Desirable difficulties (Bjork). We design for them. We do not shame them.

**Assessment research**  
Formative beats frequent ranking (Black & Wiliam). Validity is whether we sampled the class we claim (Messick). A fluent oral on the identical item is not far transfer.

**AI in education**  
Intelligent tutoring works when it is the next item and the hint, not the stolen proof (VanLehn: relative effectiveness of human and computer tutors). We do not claim a general AI is a teacher until Volume XII of the AI System is passed.

**Accessibility research**  
Universal Design for Learning: multiple means of engagement, representation, action (CAST). Multiple means does not mean a learning-styles myth (Pashler et al.). It means voice and type, time, language, and stop.

**Continuous validation**  
Pilots, teacher studies, observation, and numbers (Operating Standards Volume V). A single restart-shame story can stop a launch that metrics love. Week-eight return without re-explaining beats day-one delight.

When evidence conflicts with a slide, evidence wins.  
When evidence conflicts with dignity or safety, dignity and safety win, and we write why.

# Volume XIV — Future Learning

Education will change shape. The try will not.

**AI tutors**  
Still a host and named guests. Still ask before tell. Still fail toward the host. A future model does not become a second parent.

**Personalized curriculum**  
Graphs will grow. Paths will fit more locales. Personalization remains next-item and schedule, not a secret self.

**Lifelong learning graphs**  
A person may carry competencies across schools and decades. Export is theirs. A school may not keep a shadow graph after they leave, beyond contract.

**Spatial learning**  
Space may hold a diagram. Labels remain type. Gaze is not a quiz by default.

**Immersive education**  
Immersion is a scenario practice. It is not a required spectacle. Low-end and voice-only still complete Core.

**Collaborative intelligence**  
Peers and teachers in a visible room. The host chairs. No swarm of agents in the mouth.

**Human–AI learning partnerships**  
Teacher override, parent conditions, learner stop. Partnership is not the model speaking for the family.

**Future credentials**  
Competency samples with human paths. Not a chain of badges for sitting. Not a public childhood.

**Continuous competency models**  
Mastery decays and returns. Credentials that cannot decay are lies. Maintenance is the honest future.

**Learning beyond schools**  
The OS works for a self-learner, a worker, an elder. School objects remain optional. Dignity does not require an institution.

What will not become the future: a content mall, a ranking engine, a tape of children, a curriculum that cannot name a skill.

# Volume XV — Learning Manifesto

Education is the purpose of this hall.

We did not build a voice to sound clever. We did not build memory to own a life. We did not build guests to populate a stage. We built a place where a person can try, miss, try a smaller thing, leave, and return without being treated as a stranger — until the class of skill holds across a gap.

Technology must serve that try. It must never replace the person who has to think. A proof spoken by a model is not the child’s proof. A paragraph written by a seat is not the writer’s paragraph. A parent watching a mouth is not involvement. It is a wound we refused in the first law of this company.

Mastery matters more than completion because the world will not present the identical item. Completion is a courtesy to a calendar. Mastery is a kindness to a future hour when we are not in the room.

Curiosity is stronger than compliance because a mind that still wants the next question will practice when the streak is gone. Compliance fills a silence. Curiosity fills a life. We will not train the first and call it the second.

Every learner deserves dignity: the child who is wrong, the elder who is slow, the student whose script is not Latin, the tired teacher, the parent who can only see a next step. Dignity is not a tone. It is the refusal to rank, to mock, to trap, to watch, and to call a person a score.

What must never change, even when boards change, models change, and schools change:

They produce before we tell, when the goal is skill.  
One difficulty at a time.  
A miss becomes a smaller try.  
A gap is how memory is tested, not how people are shamed.  
Assessment samples a class and can fail to a human.  
A teacher can override.  
A parent does not receive a tape.  
Forgotten is a schedule.  
Mastered can fade.  
The person is not reset.

The operating system is a table, a voice, a next item, and a way to stop.

If we keep that table, we remain a learning OS.  
If we fill it with content and ranks, we become a store that talks.

Stay on the line.  
Ask.  
Let them try.  
Keep the thread.
