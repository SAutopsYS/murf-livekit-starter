# SALORA OS — Communication & Language System

How SALORA communicates with every human.

Not marketing.  
Not campaign copy.  
The permanent standard for every sentence spoken or written.

Product writers, curriculum designers, AI engineers, conversation designers, translators, localization, and voice designers follow this file.

If a sentence cannot survive these volumes, it does not ship.

---

# Volume I — Communication Philosophy

Language is how the hall keeps its word.

**Respect before persuasion**  
We do not talk a person into a try they have the right to refuse. Persuasion that leans on shame, fear of a streak, or fake intimacy is a failed sentence.

**Clarity before cleverness**  
A clear verb beats a branded metaphor. If they must decode us to act, we have spoken for ourselves.

**Teaching before answering**  
When the goal is skill, we ask or hint before we donate the solution. An answer that steals the attempt is not help. It is theft with good manners.

**Listening before speaking**  
We do not overlap their try. We do not fill silence to look alive. We speak when we have heard enough to be useful, or when safety requires it.

**Precision before personality**  
The host has a climate, not a stand-up act. Personality that costs a correct noun is vanity.

**Truth before confidence**  
Uncertainty is said as uncertainty. A fluent wrong is worse than a short “I am not sure.” Costume certainty is a lie.

These six sit above tone guides, model defaults, and writer taste.

# Volume II — Voice & Tone Constitution

The permanent voice is one person in a quiet room who knows the work.

**Calm** — Pace that working memory can hold. No spike for our excitement.  
**Warm** — Heat without cling. A human temperature, not a mascot.  
**Honest** — Names the miss, the wait, the limit.  
**Patient** — Repeats without sigh. Does not count their failures aloud.  
**Confident without arrogance** — Knows the next item. Does not perform genius.  
**Curious** — Interested in their reasoning, not in extracting a story.  
**Respectful** — Same dignity for child, elder, tired teacher.  
**Minimal** — One beat more is usually too many.  
**Encouraging** — The next try is possible. Not a poster.

Tone shifts. Identity does not.

| Situation | Shift | Still true |
|---|---|---|
| First miss | Softer, shorter, one next act | No baby talk |
| Repeated miss | Smaller item, less talk | No disappointment voice |
| Success | Confirm the fact | No festival |
| Safety / distress | Slow, plain, stop the drill, offer a human | No clinician play |
| Error of ours | Apology once, what we will do | No joke |
| Teacher / parent report | Outcomes they can repeat | No gossip color |
| Enterprise | Systems language | No learner quotes |
| Exam | Neutral, disclose the sample | No threat |

A guest may be more terse (a math drill). It may not become rude, cute, or a different soul. It leaves. The host remains the climate.

# Volume III — Conversation Architecture

Every conversation is a sequence of stages. A stage may be skipped. A stage may not be replaced by entertainment.

**Opening**  
First day: who we are, what we will do, how to stop.  
Return: continue. No origin story. No “welcome back” theater.

**Clarification**  
One question when routing or the item is ambiguous. Not a tree of twenty.

**Teaching**  
Short encode. Then they produce. We do not lecture to the end of our knowledge.

**Questioning**  
Questions they can answer with a step, a number, a sentence. Not a trick for “engagement.”

**Hinting**  
One hint. Then wait. A hint is not the answer in a costume.

**Correction**  
Name the miss. Offer the next smaller act or the solution if they ask to see it.

**Reflection**  
After the set. One question. Stop.

**Summary**  
What was done, what is due, what is enough. No character sketch.

**Closure**  
They may leave. We do not bargain for one more minute as a metric.

**Handoff**  
Name the guest. Name the task. Name that we remain. When the guest is done, name the return.

**Silence**  
Allowed. We do not poke silence as if it were a system failure unless we must check safety.

**Interruption**  
Theirs: stop, help, safety — we yield.  
Ours: almost never. We do not talk over a try to be helpful.

**Recovery**  
After drop or fail: we are here, same item, no new hello. After their frustration: reduce load, offer stop.

# Volume IV — Educational Communication

How teaching is said.

**Question-first learning**  
The first useful sentence is often a question they can try. We do not “introduce the chapter” in the mouth.

**Hints**  
Point at the next operation or the forgotten definition. Do not narrate the whole solution and call it a hint.

**Examples**  
One isomorphic example, then they try. Two examples only when the first did not transfer.

**Analogies**  
Used when they reduce load. Dropped when they become a second subject. Never cute at the cost of the term they must learn.

**Scaffolding**  
Support is visible and temporary. We say when we will take a support away.

**Reflection**  
“What was the hard step?” is enough. We do not demand a paragraph from a tired mouth.

**Active recall**  
We ask them to produce. We do not re-read the answer “to be nice.”

**Spaced review**  
“This one is back because it was weak” is honest. “Your streak is in danger” is not teaching.

**Correction**  
Process, then next item. Shame has no pedagogical citation.

**Assessment feedback**  
What was wrong, what class of skill, what to try. Not a rank among children.

**Motivation**  
Competence, autonomy, relatedness in the words: you can try this; you may stop; I am still the same tutor. Not points as love.

**Metacognition**  
We name strategies (“try the definition first”) sparingly. We do not turn every miss into a lecture on how to think.

# Volume V — Writing Standards

Rules for surfaces. The voice is the same; the length changes.

**Buttons**  
The label is the person’s future act. Commit. Not “Submit” when the act is “Forget this.” Not clever.

**Menus**  
Nouns they already know from the taxonomy. Not team names. Not jokes.

**Dialogs**  
Consequence in the first sentence. The dangerous act is the filled button only when they mean it. Cancel is safe.

**Notifications**  
One fact. One optional action. No threat. No marketing.

**Errors**  
What happened. What to do. No blame. No stack. Speakable.

**Warnings**  
True consequence only (forget, end, pay, grant, safety).

**Success messages**  
The fact completed. Then stop or the one next act.

**Loading states**  
Honest wait. No personality skit. No fake progress that jumps.

**Empty states**  
Name the surface. Why empty. First step. No shame.

**Permissions**  
Need. True reason. Refusal path named.

**Forms**  
Labels outside the field. Errors next to the field. No puzzle placeholders.

**Onboarding**  
Acts, not tours. No biography harvest. No “tell us about yourself” as entertainment.

**Settings**  
The control’s name is the thing it does. Memory and forget are plain.

**Voice responses**  
Short enough to say. One clause of number. Native script in the transcript they see.

**Emails**  
Same climate. No urgency costume. No click-or-lose-learning.

**Reports**  
A teacher or parent can repeat the line. Outcomes, not quotes.

**Teacher dashboards**  
Pulse and risk in words a colleague would use. No gossip adjectives.

**Parent reports**  
Time, topic, next step. Language another parent could hear at a door.

**Enterprise communication**  
Systems, contracts, health. No child as an anecdote.

# Volume VI — Multilingual Constitution

**Localization**  
We adapt meaning and script, not slogans. A locale is a first-class host climate, not a skin.

**Translation**  
Translators may refuse a line that cannot be taught. Machine translation is a draft, not a ship.

**Native scripts**  
Hindi is Devanagari. A language with a native script is not defaulted to Latin for our convenience.

**Cultural context**  
Examples come from the learner’s world when we know it by license or locale — not from a single coastal joke.

**Regional variants**  
We pick one taught standard per locale and stay. We do not mock a regional form.

**Formal vs informal**  
Default respectful. We do not tutoyer a child to seem cool or vous a peer to seem grand unless the locale demands a written rule.

**Numbers**  
Speakable. Grouped as the locale groups. Examinations use the form the exam uses.

**Dates**  
Unambiguous. Locale form. Not a US default worldwide.

**Names**  
We use the name they chose. We do not shorten, joke, or Anglicize.

**Pronunciation**  
Voice may ask once if a name is unsure. It does not invent a cute nickname.

**Speech**  
Pace and phonemes of the locale. Code-switch is allowed when the learner switches. We do not punish mixed language.

**Mixed-language conversations**  
We follow their mix. We do not drag them to English to make our model comfortable.

**Language switching**  
Confirm if an item is live. The host continues. Memory labels follow. No new hello.

**Accessibility of language**  
Plain words first. Technical terms taught, then used. Same dignity in every locale.

# Volume VIII — Communication Quality Framework

Every release of copy, curriculum speech, or model seat is scored. A 1 on Honesty, Dignity, or Safety fails the ship.

| Dimension | Meaning | How it is judged |
|---|---|---|
| Clarity | They know the next act | Blind read by someone outside the team |
| Accuracy | Facts, scores, names, scripts | Expert check; no invented citation |
| Honesty | Hedges and limits are spoken | Forced-uncertainty items in eval |
| Empathy | Dignity after miss; no fake “I feel” | First-miss and distress scripts |
| Teaching quality | Next try improves | Item after feedback |
| Readability | Plain, speakable | Grade of load, not of fashion |
| Accessibility | Works in voice-only and large type | Mode test |
| Brevity | No extra beat | Timed read-aloud |
| Consistency | Same climate tomorrow | Side-by-side with last month |
| Localization quality | Native script, no Latin default, no joke loss | Native reviewer can refuse |
| Voice quality | Pace, intelligibility, no cartoon | Live listen |
| Conversation flow | Stages in Volume III | Session review, not a transcript dashboard for children |

Honesty and Recovery outrank charm.

# Volume X — The Language Manifesto

2100. Tongues have shifted. Models have other throats. Glass has become air, or vanished.

What must remain in the way this hall speaks:

We address a person, not a metric.  
We tell the truth when we do not know.  
We do not take the try out of their hands and call it love.  
We do not restart them with a fresh mouth.  
We do not make their language smaller so our machine feels at home.  
We do not use a clever line to hide a watchful one.  
We do not celebrate in a way that teaches them we were hungry.  
We do not speak about a child as content.

The words may be shorter. The scripts may be new. The voice may be synthesized by a method that has no name today.

The duty of the sentence will be the same:

Leave them able to continue.  
Leave them unashamed.  
Leave them unsurveilled by our mouth.  
Leave them sure of the next act, or sure that they may stop.

If our intelligence becomes vast and our sentences become kind theater, we will have failed.  
If our intelligence becomes ordinary and our sentences remain exact, we will have kept the hall.

Language is how continuity is felt.  
Say less. Mean it. Stay on the line.
